The idea

A road trip you play through.

Most pickleball trips are a resort with courts attached. This one moves. You board in Chicago with thirty-one other players and step off at the Pacific ten days later, having played your way across eight states with dozens of different partners and opponents.

The draw is not made before you arrive. Seeds come from watching you play on Day 2, and the completed bracket goes up on an easel in the hotel lobby that same evening.

Nobody sits out. Players knocked out of the bracket still play every session — clinics and recreational play with a local club pro in every region.

Your travel partner does not have to play. Up to eight non-playing companion seats ride, stay and eat with the tour on every departure.

The pickleball

Two brackets. Nine mornings. Nobody on the sideline.

You register alone — no partner required. Self-reported DUPR sizes the field; it does not set the draw. The directors seed from what they watch at the Day-2 session, pair one with thirty-two, two with thirty-one, and post the finished bracket at that night's check-in.

Two bracketed formats run the length of the route: team doubles, where you keep your partner, and blind doubles, where partners are drawn fresh each round. Between them you play with and against thirty-odd different people.

The bracket splits across two resorts — round of 16 and quarterfinals at the first, semifinals and finals at the second, on one court with the whole tour watching. Singles runs informally on resort free time: self-reported results, a bragging-rights table, no schedule.
